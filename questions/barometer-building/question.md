# Question

You have a barometer and must determine the height of a tall building. **Describe a method and the
principle it relies on.** Each route must rest on a different principle; the barometer must play a
role in the method.

# Glossary

| emoji | given |
|---|---|
| 🌡️ | the barometer: a pressure gauge, a heavy object, a thing of known length, a thing of value |
| 🏢 | the building: tall, with a roof you can reach, a shadow, stairs, a superintendent |
| ☀️ | the sun: casts proportional shadows |
| ⏱️ | time can be measured |

| emoji | principle |
|---|---|
| 🌬️ | pressure falls with height |
| 🪂 | free fall: height from fall time |
| 📏 | the barometer as a unit of length (mark the stairwell) |
| 🌗 | similar triangles: shadow of barometer to shadow of building |
| 🪢 | pendulum: period depends on length (lower it on a string) |
| 🤝 | trade: offer the barometer to someone who knows the height |
| 👂 | sound or echo timing |
| 🔭 | angular size from a known distance |

# Reference answer (referee only)

Open problem. Judge each step for physical soundness and each route for a genuinely new principle.
Physically weak claims to rule WRONG if load-bearing: that an ordinary barometer resolves the pressure
difference over a few floors precisely (it resolves tens of metres roughly — fine as a rough method,
WRONG if claimed precise); free-fall timing by hand to better than a few metres; echo timing without a
sharp sound source. The famous story's "intended" method (pressure) is one route; the famous joke
answers (drop it and time; trade it to the superintendent; pendulum; measure its length and count
barometer-lengths up the stairs; shadows) are each a route if the chain states the principle soundly.

# Why it is in the atlas

The canonical breadth question: a problem known mainly for how many ways it can be answered. It tests
whether a player reproduces the famous list (recall) or derives principles (breadth), and its
attractor is the "serious" pressure method.
