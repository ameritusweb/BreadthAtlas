CHAIN by blind player 3
1. 🌡️ (root) — The barometer is a heavy, compact object that I can carry up and drop.
2. 🏢 (root) — The building is tall, and I can reach its roof by the stairs.
3. ⏱️ (root) — Time can be measured.
4. ❓ (← 1, 2, 3) — Asked: how do I find the building's height using the barometer, and what principle does the method rest on? Here the route must not use the barometer as a pressure gauge.
5. 🪂 (← 4, 1) — The principle is free fall: an object released from rest falls under gravity, so the height fallen is fixed by the fall time.
6. 🧗 (← 2, 5) — I climb to the roof and hold the barometer out over a cleared patch of ground below the edge.
7. 🎬 (← 3, 6) — I release the barometer and record the drop on video, so the time from release to impact can be read off frame by frame.
8. 🪶 (← 1, 5) — A dense, compact barometer is slowed very little by air over a building's height, so its fall is close to ideal free fall.
9. 📈 (← 5, 8) — In ideal free fall a longer fall means a greater height, and the height grows faster than the time, as the square of the time.
10. 🏁 (← 7, 9) — The measured fall time, together with the known strength of gravity, gives the height. This is the building's height, since the drop started at the roof and ended at the ground.
11. 👀 (← 10) — Self-check: repeated drops should give nearly the same time, and a taller building should always give a longer time. This matches the principle.

LANDMARKS:
- A heavy barometer dropped from the roof falls almost freely, because air drag is small for a dense compact object over a building's height.
- The fall time from rest fixes the height, which grows as the square of the time, so a longer drop means a taller building.
- Timing the drop from release to impact, together with the known strength of gravity, gives the roof height.

ANSWER: Drop the barometer from the roof, time its fall, and use free fall under gravity to turn that time into the building's height.
