4. 🌍 (root) — Given: the world's population is about eight billion people.

7. 🏊🏊 (← 1, 6) — That daily total for all of humanity fills several thousand Olympic pools, so the whole world drains a few thousand pools of drinking water every single day.
8. 📆 (← 3, 7) — Several thousand pools a day, kept up for the tens of thousands of days in one lifetime, comes to a couple of hundred million pools drunk by humanity in one lifetime.
9. ⚖️ (← 4, 8) — A couple of hundred million pools is still far fewer than eight billion people, and the people outnumber the pools by a factor of about forty.
10. 🍰 (← 9) — Shared fairly, each person's lifetime part of humanity's drinking is therefore only about a fortieth of one pool.
11. 💥 (← 9, 10) — For a typical person to drink a whole pool, there would have to be as many pools as people, which contradicts the forty-fold surplus of people over pools.
12. 🤪 (← 2, 10) — A very heavy drinker who takes a few times the usual amount only raises their share from a fortieth to around a tenth of a pool.
13. 👀 (← 11, 12) — Self-check: even the heavy drinker's share stays a small fraction of one pool, so the verdict does not depend on assuming normal habits.
14. 🚫 (← 10, 12, 13) — So no single person could drink a pool's worth of water in one lifetime.
15. 🔢 (← 9, 14) — The people-to-pools surplus shows that the pool is bigger than one lifetime of drinking by a factor in the tens, roughly forty.
LANDMARKS:
- All of humanity together drinks several thousand Olympic pools of water every day.
- Over one lifetime, humanity drinks a couple of hundred million pools, which is about forty times fewer pools than there are people.
- Each person's share of humanity's lifetime drinking is only about a fortieth of a pool, and even a heavy drinker reaches only about a tenth.
ANSWER: No; the pool is bigger by roughly 40 times.
