1. 🏊 (root) — Given: an Olympic pool holds a few million litres, about what a household would need for many years.
2. 🥤 (root) — Given: a person drinks roughly a couple of litres of water a day.
3. 🧓 (root) — Given: a long lifetime is around eighty years, which is tens of thousands of days.
4. 🚿 (root) — Given: in a wealthy country a person's total household water use (showers, toilets, laundry, cooking, dishes, garden) is on the order of a couple of hundred litres a day.
5. 🔍 (← 2, 4) — Drinking is therefore only about one part in a hundred of everyday household water use, a tiny sliver of it.
6. 🏠 (← 3, 4) — Over a whole lifetime, household use adds up to a few million litres, which is the same order as one pool, perhaps a few pools.
7. 🤝 (← 1, 6) — So a pool is roughly what one person's entire lifetime of water use amounts to, and that total already includes every shower and flush.
8. ✂️ (← 5, 7) — The drinking slice of that lifetime total is about one hundredth of it, so a lifetime of drinking is only a small fraction of a pool.
9. 🧯 (← 8) — The remaining water in a lifetime's use goes to washing and flushing, none of which counts as drinking, so it cannot be added to the drinking total.
10. 💥 (← 7, 9) — If one person drank the pool, they would have to drink all of the water a person normally uses in a lifetime, which is about a hundred times more than they drink. That contradicts the fact that drinking is only a tiny share of use.
11. 👀 (← 5, 10) — Self-check: the argument rests on the drinking share being about a hundredth, and it still works if that share is a bit larger. Even if a heavy drinker triples the intake, the share stays at a few percent, far from the whole.
12. 🚫 (← 8, 10, 11) — So no single person could drink a pool's worth of water in one lifetime.
13. 🔢 (← 1, 8, 12) — The pool is bigger than the lifetime drinking total by a factor in the tens, roughly forty.

LANDMARKS:
- A person's entire lifetime of household water use (washing, flushing, cooking) is about one pool, perhaps a few pools.
- Drinking is only about a hundredth of everyday household water use.
- Drinking the pool would mean drinking an amount equal to a lifetime of all domestic use, which is about a hundred times the actual drinking.

ANSWER: No; the pool is bigger by roughly 40 times.
