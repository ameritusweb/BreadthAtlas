CHAIN by extender
1. 🏊 (root) — Given: an Olympic pool is about fifty metres long, twenty-five metres wide and two metres deep, and it holds a few million litres.
2. 🥤 (root) — Given: a person drinks roughly a couple of litres of water a day over a long lifetime of about eighty years, which is tens of thousands of days.
3. ❓ (← 1, 2) — Asked: could one person drink as much water as an Olympic pool holds over a whole lifetime? Answer yes or no, and say roughly how many times larger the bigger amount is than the smaller.
4. 🗓️ (← 2) — A couple of litres a day over tens of thousands of days adds up to tens of thousands of litres, which is a few tens of cubic metres because a cubic metre holds a thousand litres.
5. 🟦 (← 1) — The pool's floor, fifty metres by twenty-five, covers well over a thousand square metres, so it is a very wide surface.
6. 📏 (← 4, 5) — A few tens of cubic metres poured into the empty pool and spread across more than a thousand square metres would make a layer only a few centimetres deep, barely enough to wet your ankles.
7. 🏊‍♂️ (← 1, 6) — The full pool is two metres deep, a couple of hundred centimetres, so it is tens of times deeper than the few-centimetre layer from a lifetime of drinking.
8. ☀️ (root) — Given: an uncovered outdoor pool in summer sun loses roughly half a centimetre of depth a day to evaporation.
9. 🌫️ (← 6, 8) — A layer a few centimetres deep, lost at about half a centimetre a day, would evaporate in about a week or two. In other words, the sun takes a lifetime of one person's drinking off the pool's surface in under a fortnight.
10. 🤪 (← 2, 6) — A very heavy drinker who takes a few times the usual amount only deepens the layer a few times, to about a hand's breadth, which is still a small part of two metres.
11. 👀 (← 7, 10) — Self-check: even the heavy drinker's hand's-breadth layer is far below the two-metre waterline, so the verdict does not depend on assuming normal habits.
12. 🚫 (← 7, 9, 10) — So no single person could drink a pool's worth of water in one lifetime, since their whole life's drinking only coats the floor and the sun could evaporate it in days.
13. 🔢 (← 7, 12) — A few centimetres compared with two metres shows the pool is bigger by a factor in the tens, roughly forty.
LANDMARKS:
- A lifetime of drinking poured into the empty pool covers the floor only a few centimetres deep.
- The full pool is two metres deep, tens of times deeper than that layer.
- In summer, evaporation from the pool's surface takes away a lifetime of one person's drinking in about a week or two.
- Even a very heavy drinker would fill the pool only about a hand's breadth deep.
ANSWER: No; the pool is bigger by roughly 40 times.
