1. 🏊 — An Olympic pool is a huge rectangular tank, about fifty metres long, roughly two metres deep and many lanes wide.
2. 🛁 (← 1) — Measured in bathtubs, the pool holds on the order of ten thousand of them.
3. 🥤 — A person drinks only a couple of litres a day, about a few big bottles' worth.
4. 🪣 (← 3) — A bathtub holds far more than a day's drinking, so it would take a person many weeks to drink one tub.
5. 📅 — A long lifetime is about eighty years, which is a few thousand weeks.
6. 🧮 (← 4, 5) — Weeks of drinking per tub set against a few thousand weeks of life means a lifetime of drinking fills only a few hundred tubs.
7. 🛁 (← 2, 6) — Ten thousand tubs against a few hundred tubs shows that the pool is much bigger than the lifetime total.
8. 🍵 — Food and other drinks add some water, but they are not plain drinking water, so I count them as extra.
9. 🏃 (← 3, 5) — A heavy drinker might drink two or three times the usual amount, and that still leaves the lifetime total in the low thousands of tubs at most.
10. 👀 (← 7, 9) — Self-check: even tripling the intake leaves the lifetime total well short of the pool.
11. 🚫 (← 7, 10) — Therefore no person could drink an Olympic pool's worth of water in one lifetime.
12. 🔢 (← 6, 2, 11) — The pool is bigger than the lifetime total by a factor in the tens, about forty.

LANDMARKS:
- An Olympic pool is about ten thousand bathtubs.
- A lifetime of drinking is a few hundred bathtubs.
- Even a heavy drinker with triple the intake stays far below the pool.

ANSWER: No; the pool is bigger by roughly 40 times.
