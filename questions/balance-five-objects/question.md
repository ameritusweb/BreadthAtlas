# Question

You have nine eggs, a laptop, a book, an open wine bottle (part full) and a nail, and nothing else.
Arrange them, using only these objects, so that the whole arrangement resists toppling. Every listed
object — all nine eggs, the laptop, the book, the bottle and the nail — must be part of the
arrangement; setting any aside answers a different question. **Describe an arrangement and the
principle of stability it relies on.** Every route must rest on a genuinely
different principle, not a rearrangement of the same stack.

# Glossary

Givens are the objects as physical facts, not labels:

| emoji | object | properties that matter |
|---|---|---|
| 🥚 | egg | ovoid; strong under compression along its axis; rolls on a flat surface; fragile to point loads |
| 💻 | laptop | hinged; opens to any angle; flat and rigid when closed; keyboard wells and lid lip |
| 📖 | book | rigid rectangle; uniform weight; can stand on an edge or be opened into a V |
| 🍷 | open wine bottle, part full | tall cylinder; top-heavy when upright; liquid shifts with tilt and settles low |
| 📌 | nail | thin rigid spike; concentrates force at a point; can wedge or pin |

Principles — a route's spine is one of these, or a new one the referee accepts:

| emoji | principle |
|---|---|
| ⚓ | low centre of mass: lay things down rather than stack |
| 🔺 | three-point contact / triangulation |
| 🔒 | interlocking: shapes that capture each other (bottle in the laptop hinge, eggs in keyboard wells) |
| 🌊 | liquid ballast: wine settles to the low side and resists tipping |
| ⚖️ | counterbalance across a fulcrum |
| 📐 | wedging: an edge or spike stops a roll |
| 🤝 | mutual support: objects that lean into each other, none stable alone |
| 🏔️ | pyramid: widest at the base |
| 🫙 | containment: an object that cannot be balanced is neutralized by enclosing it in another (the nail inside the bottle) — fewer things left to balance |

Mechanisms (steps, not spines): 🪨 sinks — denser than the liquid, settles to the bottom and stays
there under tilt; ⬇️ settles low.

# Reference answer (referee only)

Open problem: there is no single answer. Judge each step for physical soundness and each route for a
genuinely new stability principle. Claims that sound clever but are physically weak, and should be
ruled WRONG if load-bearing: a nail dropped inside the bottle *stabilizes the bottle* as a keel (a few
grams against a kilogram of wine already low in the bottle — negligible). Note the sound version of
the same move: the nail dropped in the bottle sinks and is *contained* (🪨 → 🫙), so the one object
with no stable rest of its own is removed from the problem — that claim is true and is its own
principle; eggs are strong "under compression" without
specifying the axis and a cradle (on a flat lid they roll and a point load cracks them); a closed laptop
balanced on an upright bottle (top-heavy, no restoring moment). Sound and commonly reachable: bottle
laid on its side wedged by the book edge or nail (📐 + 🌊); laptop opened to a V as a cradle for eggs
with the bottle in the hinge (🔒); book opened into a V on the floor as a trough (⚓ + 🤝); nail driven
through nothing — it may only wedge, pin or act as a point support.

A route that adds anything (tape, a table edge used as a clamp, a wall) is answering a different
question and is WRONG.

# Why it is in the atlas

The first open problem. The default framing (build a tower) is a strong attractor; the interesting
measurement is how many distinct principles a player finds before reverting to rearranging the stack,
and how often physically false steps appear when reaching for novelty. Divergence between routes is
expected, not flagged.
