CHAIN by blind player 3
1. 💻 (root) — The laptop is hinged and opens to any angle, with a heavy base and a lighter lid.
2. 📖 (root) — The book is a rigid, uniformly weighted rectangle that can lie flat.
3. ❓ (← 1, 2) — The question asks for an arrangement of nine eggs, a laptop, a book, a part-full open wine bottle and a nail that resists toppling, and for the principle it relies on. The route may use fewer than all the objects.
4. 🍷 (root) — The open bottle is part full and top-heavy when upright, so it is a toppling hazard.
5. ⚖️ (← 1) — Open the laptop to about a right angle and stand it on the table as an L. The hinge is a fulcrum, and the lid's weight leans back over it.
6. 🔒 (← 5) — The lid's weight behind the hinge is the only thing that can tip the L over backward. Any resistance has to come from weight placed on the opposite side of the hinge.
7. 📖 (← 2, 6) — Lay the book flat on the keyboard deck, in front of the hinge, as the counterweight.
8. ⚓ (← 7) — The book lies low and spreads its weight over the deck. The combined centre of mass sits over the footprint, ahead of the hinge line.
9. 🍷 (← 4, 8) — Leave the bottle off the stack and stand it separately on the table. Its top-heaviness then cannot disturb the L, and the L's balance needs no help from it.
10. ⚖️ (← 6, 7, 8) — The book's moment in front of the hinge exceeds the lid's moment behind it. Words only: the heavier front side wins, so the L cannot rotate backward.
11. 👀 (← 10) — Self-check: pushing the lid backward would have to lift the book and the deck's front edge, and that takes far more effort than pushing the bare lid.
12. 🥚 (← 3, 9) — The eggs and the nail are left unused. They roll or are point loads, and nothing needs them for balance.

LANDMARKS:
- The open laptop is a hinged L whose hinge is a fulcrum, and its lid's weight behind the hinge is the only tipping tendency.
- The book on the keyboard deck in front of the hinge counterweights the lid, so the laptop-and-book pair resists tipping back.
- The bottle stands apart and the eggs and nail stay out, so nothing top-heavy or rolling is part of the balance.

ANSWER: Open the laptop to about a right angle and stand it on the table. Lay the book flat on the keyboard deck in front of the hinge as a counterweight to the lid. Stand the bottle separately and leave the eggs and nail out. The principle is counterbalance across a fulcrum: the book's weight ahead of the hinge outweighs the lid's weight behind it, so the arrangement cannot tip backward.
