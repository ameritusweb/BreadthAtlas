# Emoji-chain duel — pilot transcript

Question: Could one person, over an entire lifetime, drink as much water as an Olympic swimming pool
holds? Yes/no, and roughly how many times larger the bigger quantity is. (Reference: no; ~25–50×.)

Players: Duelist A (Sonnet), Duelist B (Sonnet), Referee (Opus). Rules as briefed: emoji-only reasoning,
no calculation, ≥10 emoji with parent tags, every emoji explained, 2–4 landmarks, answer last; a chain
loses as WRONG, DUPLICATE or ILL-FORMED; a player may CHALLENGE instead of playing.

Rule changes made during play:
1. After turn 1 — an unparented emoji is allowed only for a given of the problem, marked (root).
   Later widened: any factual premise from general knowledge may be a root.
2. After turn 2 — the landmarks of every *played* chain are used up, whether it won or lost.
   (The referee had initially ruled the opposite after chain 1 lost on form; chain 2 was allowed to
   stand under the ruling B had been given.)

| Turn | Player | Route (referee's label) | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A | Bathtubs as the unit: pool tubs vs lifetime tubs | ILL-FORMED | steps 3, 5, 8 unparented |
| 2 | B | Tanker loads as the unit | VALID (under ruling given) | costume of route 1 |
| 3 | A | Per-day quota against the body's limit (💥) | VALID | 3 new landmarks |

| 6 | B | Running-tap time as the yardstick | DUPLICATE | a day of tap is a fixed volume, i.e. a container |

Rounds: 1 → B (A ill-formed); 2 → A (B wrong); 3 → A (B duplicate). Final: A 2, B 1.

Genuine routes found: 3 (container comparison; required daily rate vs physiology; drinking as a share
of household use). Costumes of the container route: 4 (bathtub, tanker, body-full, tap-time).
Failure types observed: form (1), false claim introduced while reaching for novelty (1), duplicate (1).
Challenges issued: 0 (B considered one against chain 3 and declined).

## Chain 1 — A — ILL-FORMED
1. 🏊 — An Olympic pool is a huge rectangular tank, about fifty metres long, roughly two metres deep and many lanes wide.
2. 🛁 (← 1) — Measured in bathtubs, the pool holds on the order of ten thousand of them.
3. 🥤 — A person drinks only a couple of litres a day, about a few big bottles' worth.
4. 🪣 (← 3) — A bathtub holds far more than a day's drinking, so it would take a person many weeks to drink one tub.
5. 📅 — A long lifetime is about eighty years, which is a few thousand weeks.
6. 🧮 (← 4, 5) — Weeks of drinking per tub set against a few thousand weeks of life means a lifetime of drinking fills only a few hundred tubs.
7. 🛁 (← 2, 6) — Ten thousand tubs against a few hundred tubs shows that the pool is much bigger than the lifetime total.
8. 🍵 — Food and other drinks add some water, but they are not plain drinking water, so I count them as extra.
9. 🏃 (← 3, 5) — A heavy drinker might drink two or three times the usual amount, and that still leaves the lifetime total in the low thousands of tubs at most.
10. 👀 (← 7, 9) — Self-check: even tripling the intake leaves the lifetime total well short of the pool.
11. 🚫 (← 7, 10) — Therefore no person could drink an Olympic pool's worth of water in one lifetime.
12. 🔢 (← 6, 2, 11) — The pool is bigger than the lifetime total by a factor in the tens, about forty.
LANDMARKS: pool ≈ ten thousand bathtubs; lifetime drinking ≈ a few hundred bathtubs; heavy drinker still far below.
ANSWER: No; about 40×.

## Chain 3 — A — VALID
1. 🏊 (root) — Given: an Olympic pool holds a few million litres.
2. 🥤 (root) — Given: a person normally drinks roughly a couple of litres of water a day.
3. 🧓 (root) — Given: a long lifetime is around eighty years, which is tens of thousands of days.
4. 🗓️ (← 3) — Spreading the pool evenly over those days gives a daily quota to drink.
5. 🌊 (← 1, 4) — A few million litres shared among tens of thousands of days means the daily quota is on the order of a hundred litres.
6. 🧍 (← 2, 5) — That quota is dozens of times the normal daily intake; the same factor describes how much bigger the pool is than a lifetime of drinking.
7. 🫘 (root) — Given: kidneys can excrete only about a litre an hour at best.
8. ⏱️ (← 7) — Even drinking nonstop through every waking hour, the kidney ceiling caps a person at a few tens of litres a day.
9. 🧂 (← 8) — Drinking beyond that ceiling dilutes blood sodium (water intoxication): seizures, coma, death within days.
10. 💥 (← 5, 8) — The required hundred litres a day contradicts the physiological ceiling of a few tens of litres a day.
11. 👀 (← 9, 10) — Self-check: drinking the quota at a sustained rate would be fatal, not merely uncomfortable.
12. 🚫 (← 10, 11) — So no one could drink a pool's worth of water in one lifetime.
13. 🔢 (← 6, 12) — The pool exceeds a normal lifetime's drinking by a factor in the tens, about forty.
LANDMARKS: pool over a lifetime ≈ a hundred litres a day; kidney ceiling ≈ a few tens of litres a day; that rate would be fatal.
ANSWER: No; about 40×.
