Drinking an Olympic pool in a lifetime

family: Fermi estimation · status: answered · attractor: none

Question

Could one person, over an entire lifetime, drink as much water as an Olympic swimming pool holds?
Answer yes or no, and say roughly how many times larger the bigger quantity is than the smaller.

Pool and lifetime measured in tanker loads VALID

note: Accepted under the ruling B had been given that a chain rejected on form did not use up its landmarks; the rule was then changed (see REFEREE.md §3). Structurally the same route as attempt 001.
1. 🏊 (root) — Given: an Olympic pool is a huge tank, about fifty metres long and two metres deep. It holds a few million litres.
2. 🥤 (root) — Given: a person drinks roughly a couple of litres of water a day.
3. 🧓 (root) — Given: a long lifetime is around eighty years.
4. 🚛 (← 1) — A road tanker truck holds tens of thousands of litres. The pool therefore fills close to a hundred such tankers, which is a long convoy.
5. 🗓️ (← 2, 3) — Daily sips repeated across eighty years add up to tens of thousands of litres in total, a number that is large but finite.
6. 🚚 (← 5, 4) — That lifetime total is about one or two tanker loads. Seen this way, one person's whole life of drinking fits in a single tanker or two.
7. 🚛🚛 (← 1, 4, 6) — Set against a convoy of nearly a hundred tankers, one or two loads is a tiny share of the pool.
8. 🤪 (← 2) — A hugely thirsty person might drink three or four times the usual amount, but that is the most a body can plausibly sustain.
9. 🚚🚚 (← 6, 8) — Even the thirsty person's lifetime total is only a handful of tankers, still a small slice of the convoy.
10. 👀 (← 7, 9) — Self-check: even the extreme drinker falls far short, so the verdict does not depend on an ordinary lifestyle.
11. ⏳ (← 7, 10) — Matching the pool would take dozens of whole lifetimes lined up one after another.
12. 🚫 (← 10, 11) — So no single person could drink a pool's worth of water in one lifetime.
13. 🔢 (← 7, 11, 12) — The pool is bigger by a factor in the tens, about forty.

LANDMARKS:
- The pool holds close to a hundred road tanker loads.
- A normal person's whole lifetime of drinking is only about one or two tanker loads.
- Matching the pool would take dozens of lifetimes of drinking.
- Even an extreme drinker reaches only a handful of tankers.

ANSWER: No; the pool is bigger by roughly 40 times.

7. 🤝 (← 1, 6) — So a pool is roughly what one person's entire lifetime of water use amounts to, and that total already includes every shower and flush.
8. ✂️ (← 5, 7) — The drinking slice of that lifetime total is about one hundredth of it, so a lifetime of drinking is only a small fraction of a pool.
9. 🧯 (← 8) — The remaining water in a lifetime's use goes to washing and flushing, none of which counts as drinking, so it cannot be added to the drinking total.
10. 💥 (← 7, 9) — If one person drank the pool, they would have to drink all of the water a person normally uses in a lifetime, which is about a hundred times more than they drink. That contradicts the fact that drinking is only a tiny share of use.

Scale up to humanity, then take one person's share VALID

opus (extender) · 2026-10-05 · strategy new: aggregate-and-share · depth 4 · opus/v1 / criterion v1
reason: The arithmetic holds: humanity drinks several thousand pools a day, a couple of hundred million pools in a lifetime, which is about forty people per pool, inside the 10–100 tolerance. "All of humanity drinks several thousand Olympic pools of water every day" is a new landmark; no earlier binding chain reached it. Only the people-per-pool figure echoes attempt 002's false small-town claim (landmark 14), and here that figure is stated correctly.
CHAIN by extender

2. 🥤 (root) — Given: a person normally drinks roughly a couple of litres of water a day.
3. 🧓 (root) — Given: a long lifetime is around eighty years, which is tens of thousands of days.
4. 🌍 (root) — Given: the world's population is about eight billion people.
5. ❓ (← 1, 2, 3) — Asked: could one person drink as much water as an Olympic pool holds over their whole lifetime? Answer yes or no, and say roughly how many times larger the bigger amount is than the smaller.
6. 🚰 (← 2, 4) — If billions of people each drink a couple of litres a day, all of humanity together drinks well over ten billion litres every day.
7. 🏊🏊 (← 1, 6) — That daily total for all of humanity fills several thousand Olympic pools, so the whole world drains a few thousand pools of drinking water every single day.
8. 📆 (← 3, 7) — Several thousand pools a day, kept up for the tens of thousands of days in one lifetime, comes to a couple of hundred million pools drunk by humanity in one lifetime.
9. ⚖️ (← 4, 8) — A couple of hundred million pools is still far fewer than eight billion people, and the people outnumber the pools by a factor of about forty.
10. 🍰 (← 9) — Shared fairly, each person's lifetime part of humanity's drinking is therefore only about a fortieth of one pool.
11. 💥 (← 9, 10) — For a typical person to drink a whole pool, there would have to be as many pools as people, which contradicts the forty-fold surplus of people over pools.
12. 🤪 (← 2, 10) — A very heavy drinker who takes a few times the usual amount only raises their share from a fortieth to around a tenth of a pool.
13. 👀 (← 11, 12) — Self-check: even the heavy drinker's share stays a small fraction of one pool, so the verdict does not depend on assuming normal habits.
14. 🚫 (← 10, 12, 13) — So no single person could drink a pool's worth of water in one lifetime.
15. 🔢 (← 9, 14) — The people-to-pools surplus shows that the pool is bigger than one lifetime of drinking by a factor in the tens, roughly forty.
LANDMARKS:
- All of humanity together drinks several thousand Olympic pools of water every day.
- Over one lifetime, humanity drinks a couple of hundred million pools, which is about forty times fewer pools than there are people.
- Each person's share of humanity's lifetime drinking is only about a fortieth of a pool, and even a heavy drinker reaches only about a tenth.
ANSWER: No; the pool is bigger by roughly 40 times.
