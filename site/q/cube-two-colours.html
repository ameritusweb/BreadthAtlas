Two-colour cubes up to rotation

family: counting under symmetry · status: answered · attractor: none

Question

Each face of a cube is painted either black or white. Two painted cubes count as the same if one can be
rotated to look exactly like the other. **How many different painted cubes are there?**

Accepted routes (4)

Group by black-face count, then mirror by colour swap VALID

sonnet (duelist A) · 2026-10-05 · strategy new: invariant-partition · depth 1 · opus/v1 / criterion v1
reason: Every step is correct and the total of 10 is exact. Since no route has been played yet, all three landmarks are new: rotation preserves the black-face count; two black faces are adjacent or opposite and three are a corner or a band; and swapping the colours mirrors the counts of 4, 5 and 6 black faces onto 2, 1 and 0.
CHAIN by duelist A
1. 🧊 (root) — A cube has six square faces, and each face is painted black or white.
2. 🔄 (root) — Two painted cubes count as the same if some rotation of one makes it look exactly like the other.
3. ❓ (← 1, 2) — How many different painted cubes are there, counting rotated copies as the same?
4. ⚫ (← 1, 3) — Sort cubes by how many faces are black. A rotation never changes that count, so cubes in different groups are always different.
5. ⚪ (← 4) — With no black faces, the cube is all white, so there is one cube.
6. 1️⃣ (← 4) — With exactly one black face, any face can be rotated to the top, so there is one cube.
7. 2️⃣ (← 4) — With two black faces, they either share an edge or sit opposite each other. Rotation preserves which case holds and can turn any pair of the same kind into any other, so there are two cubes.
8. 3️⃣ (← 4) — With three black faces, either two of them are opposite, forming a band of three in a row, or none are opposite, so all three meet at one corner. Each case is a single cube up to rotation, so there are two cubes.
9. 🔁 (← 4) — Swapping black and white on every face respects rotation. It pairs the four-black group with the two-black group, the five-black group with the one-black group, and the six-black group with the zero-black group.
10. 👀 (← 8, 9) — Self-check: the three-black group maps to itself under the swap. A corner cube turns into a corner cube, and a band cube into a band cube, so that group is not double counted.
11. 🔟 (← 5, 6, 7, 8, 9) — Add up the groups: zero, one, two and three black faces give one, one, two and two cubes. The mirrored groups with four, five and six black faces give two, one and one. Altogether there are ten.

LANDMARKS:
- Rotation never changes the number of black faces, so cubes can be sorted into groups by black-face count.
- Two black faces form either an adjacent pair or an opposite pair, and three black faces form either a corner or a band, giving two cubes each.
- Swapping the two colours mirrors the groups with four, five and six black faces onto those with two, one and zero.
ANSWER: 10

Classify cubes by the number of mixed opposite-face pairs VALID

sonnet (duelist B) · 2026-10-05 · strategy new: opposite-pair-decomposition · depth 2 · opus/v1 / criterion v1
reason: All the steps are sound and give 10. Route 001 sorted cubes by black-face count. This chain sorts them by a different rotation-invariant, so its two later landmarks are new: "the number of mixed opposite pairs (0 to 3) splits the cubes into four classes" and "the classes contain four, three, two and one cubes."
CHAIN by duelist B
1. 🧊 (root) — A cube has six square faces, and each face is painted black or white.
2. 🔄 (root) — Two painted cubes count as the same if some rotation of one makes it look exactly like the other.
3. ❓ (← 1, 2) — How many different painted cubes are there, counting rotated copies as the same?
4. ↔️ (← 1) — The six faces split into three pairs of opposite faces, one pair for each axis through the cube.
5. 🎭 (← 4) — Each opposite pair is of one of three kinds: both black, both white, or mixed (one black, one white).
6. 🔢 (← 2, 5) — A rotation carries opposite pairs to opposite pairs and keeps their kinds. So the number of mixed pairs (0, 1, 2 or 3) is unchanged, and cubes with different counts are different.
7. 0️⃣ (← 5, 6) — With no mixed pair, every pair is both black or both white. Rotations can swap the axes freely, so only the number of both-black pairs matters. That number can be 0, 1, 2 or 3, which gives four cubes.
8. 1️⃣ (← 5, 6) — With one mixed pair, the other two pairs can be both white, both black, or one of each. A half-turn about a perpendicular axis swaps the two faces of the mixed pair and keeps the other pairs in place. So the mixed pair's orientation does not matter, and this gives three cubes.
9. 2️⃣ (← 5, 6) — With two mixed pairs, the two black faces from them are adjacent, and any such adjacent arrangement can be rotated onto any other. The third pair is then both black or both white, which gives two cubes.
10. 3️⃣ (← 5, 6) — With three mixed pairs, there is one black face on each axis, so no two black faces are opposite and all three meet at a corner. Rotations carry any corner to any other, so this gives one cube.
11. 🔟 (← 7, 8, 9, 10) — The four cases are exhaustive and mutually different. They contribute four, three, two and one cubes, which total ten.

LANDMARKS:
- A cube has three pairs of opposite faces, and each pair is both black, both white, or mixed.
- The number of mixed opposite pairs (0 to 3) is unchanged by rotation, so it splits the cubes into four classes.
- The classes contain four, three, two and one cubes respectively.
ANSWER: 10

Pin a both-black axis, count belt patterns VALID

reason: All steps are sound. The six belt patterns give six distinct cubes with a both-black pair, the four cubes without one are fixed by their count of mixed pairs, and together they make 10. The landmarks "a both-black pair can be turned to the top and bottom, leaving a four-face belt to colour up to spinning" and "a belt has six patterns up to spinning, each a different cube" are new. No earlier chain split the cubes on whether they have a both-black pair.

2. 🔄 (root) — Two painted cubes count as the same if some rotation of one makes it look exactly like the other.

6. ⬛ (← 5) — Split all cubes into two sets: those with at least one both-black opposite pair, and those with none. Rotation cannot move a cube from one set to the other.
7. 🔝 (← 2, 6) — In the first set, rotate a both-black pair to the top and bottom. The only rotations that keep it there are spins about the vertical axis, so what remains is the belt of four side faces, coloured up to spinning.
8. 📿 (← 7) — A belt of four faces, up to spinning, has black count none, one, two side by side, two opposite, three, or four. That is six patterns.
9. 🧩 (← 5, 7, 8) — These six patterns give six different cubes. Black totals separate all of them except the two-black belts, and those differ in whether the belt itself holds a both-black pair. Choosing another both-black pair as the axis gives the same pattern type, so nothing is counted twice.
10. ⚪ (← 5, 6) — In the second set every pair is white or mixed, so the number of mixed pairs (0, 1, 2 or 3) is the only thing that can vary. Zero mixed pairs is all white. 11. 👀 (← 9, 10) — Self-check: the two sets are disjoint, because having a both-black pair is unchanged by rotation. Nothing is counted in both.
12. 🔟 (← 9, 10, 11) — Six cubes from the first set and four from the second make ten in total.

LANDMARKS:
- Cubes with a both-black opposite pair can be turned so that pair is on top and bottom, leaving only the four-face belt to colour up to spinning.
- A belt of four faces has six patterns up to spinning, and each gives a different cube.
- Cubes with no both-black opposite pair number four, set by whether zero, one, two or three opposite pairs are mixed.

Pin a mixed pair, count the belt, add uniform-pair cubes VALID

Its new landmark is the first one: pinning a *mixed* opposite pair black-up and white-down leaves only spins about the vertical axis, so the cubes with a mixed pair reduce to the six belt patterns.
