All roads lead to the hub, but can anyone leave?

family: directed connectivity · status: answered · attractor: a common reachable hub connects everyone to everyone

Question

A finite network of islands uses one-way bridges. There is a central island H that every island can reach by following the bridge directions. Does this guarantee that every island can reach every other island? Give a counterexample if not.

Accepted routes (3)

Sink hub: inward reachability gives no way out VALID

sonnet (blind player 1) · 2026-10-05 · strategy new: minimal-sink-counterexample · depth 1 · opus/v2 / criterion v2
reason: The two-island network with one bridge from A to H meets the givens, since A reaches H and H reaches itself trivially. In that network H cannot reach A, so the answer "no" is correct, and the chain has no calculation or simulation. Its new landmarks are that reaching the hub says nothing about leaving it, that a sink hub satisfies the givens, and that the one-bridge A→H network is a minimal counterexample.
CHAIN by blind player 1
1. 🏝️➡️🏠 (root) — Given: every island can reach the hub H by following bridge directions.
2. 🌉☝️ (root) — Given: all bridges are one-way.
3. ❓ (← 1, 2) — Asked: must every island also be able to reach every other island? 🔁🏝️🏝️ is the "reach each other" property, and a counterexample is wanted if not.
4. 🧪 (← 3) — To answer "no," it is enough to build one small network satisfying the givens where some island cannot reach another.
5. 🏠🏝️ (← 4) — Take the smallest example: hub H plus one other island A, two islands in total.
6. 🌉A➡️H (← 2, 5) — Build a single one-way bridge from A to H and no other bridge.
7. ✅1 (← 1, 6) — The givens hold: A reaches H by the bridge, and H reaches itself by staying put.
8. 🚫H➡️A (← 6) — No bridge leaves H, so from H there is no move at all and H cannot get to A.
9. 💥? (← 7, 8) — Not a contradiction: this shows the "every island reaches every other" claim fails here, with the givens still true.
10. 👀 (← 6, 8) — Self-check: the only bridge points into H, so nothing can leave H; this confirms A is unreachable from H.
11. 🔄 (← 3, 9) — The guarantee would need a way back out of H, and the givens only say that roads lead in. Nothing about them forces a road out.
12. 🎯 (← 7, 8, 11) — Conclusion: the hub-reachability property does not guarantee mutual reachability, and the two-island network with one bridge A to H is a counterexample.

LANDMARKS:
- Reaching the hub is one-directional information: it says nothing about leaving the hub.
- A hub with no outgoing bridges (a sink) satisfies the givens while being unable to reach anyone else.
- The two-island network with a single bridge A→H is a minimal counterexample.

ANSWER: No. For example, with islands A and H and one one-way bridge from A to H, every island reaches H, but H cannot reach A.

Condense into reachability clusters; hub's cluster is sink VALID

sonnet (blind player 2) · 2026-10-05 · strategy new: condensation-sink-component · depth 2 · opus/v2 / criterion v2
reason: The chain has no simulation and every step is sound. Step 12 is the chain's stated conclusion, so it does work. Its new landmarks are not among route 001's three. First, the islands split into mutual-reachability clusters whose condensation has no loops. Second, H's cluster is forced to be the dead end of that picture. Third, the guarantee holds exactly when H's cluster is the whole network.
CHAIN by blind player 2
1. 🏝️ (root) — Given: a finite set of islands joined by one-way bridges.
2. 🎯 (root) — Given: every island can reach the central island H by following bridge directions.
3. ❓ (← 1, 2) — Asked: does "all reach H" force "every island reaches every other" (🔁↔️ for every pair, not just 🎯 toward H)? If not, give a counterexample.
4. 🔄 (← 1, 3) — "Everyone reaches everyone" means mutual reachability, so islands that can reach each other form natural clusters.
5. 🧩 (← 4) — Every island belongs to exactly one cluster of mutual reachability, so the islands split cleanly into clusters.
6. ⬇️ (← 5) — Collapse each cluster to a single point. The bridges left between clusters can never form a loop, because a loop would merge those clusters into one.
7. 🕳️ (← 2, 6) — Every cluster must lead into H's cluster. If H's cluster had a bridge out to another cluster, that cluster would also be reachable from H, so the two would merge. So H's cluster is the dead end of the collapsed picture.
8. 🚪 (← 7) — The guarantee therefore holds only if H's cluster contains all the islands. Any island outside it is an entrant that can get in but never back out.
9. 🌟 (← 1, 8) — Build a concrete case: islands A, B and H, with bridges A→H and B→H only. A and B are outsiders to H's cluster.
10. ✅ (← 2, 9) — Check the given: A reaches H directly, B reaches H directly, and H trivially reaches itself, so everyone reaches H.
11. 🚫 (← 9) — No bridge leaves H, so H cannot reach A or B. A also cannot reach B, because A's only bridge ends at H, which is a dead end.
12. ⚖️ (← 3, 8, 10, 11) — The givens hold but mutual reachability fails, so the guarantee is false.
13. 👀 (← 10) — Self-check: the network is finite, the bridges are one-way, and every island reaches H, so the counterexample respects all the givens.

LANDMARKS:
- Islands split into clusters of mutual reachability, and the bridges between clusters never form a loop.
- If everyone reaches H, H's cluster is the dead end of the cluster picture, with no bridge leaving it.
- The guarantee holds exactly when H's cluster is the whole network. A three-island inward star (A→H, B→H) fails this and also leaves A and B unable to reach each other.

ANSWER: No. All islands reaching H does not make every island reach every other. Counterexample: islands A, B and H with bridges A→H and B→H only. Everyone reaches H, but H cannot reach A or B, and A cannot reach B.

One-way line: source unreachable, sink hub cannot exit VALID

sonnet (extender) · 2026-10-05 · strategy new: source-and-sink-obstruction · depth 3 · opus/v2 / criterion v2
reason: The one-way line A→B→H is a correct counterexample. Its step-by-step reasoning is structural, so it does not count as simulation. It adds a new landmark: a source island with no incoming bridge can never be reached by anyone, which is an obstruction separate from the sink hub of route 001 and the cluster dead end of route 002.
CHAIN by extender
1. 🏝️🌉☝️ (root) — Given: a finite set of islands joined only by one-way bridges.
2. 🏠👈🏝️ (root) — Given: every island can reach the central island H by following bridge directions.
3. ❓ (← 1, 2) — Asked: does "everyone reaches H" (🏝️➡️🏠) force "everyone reaches everyone" (🔁🏝️🏝️ for every pair)? If not, give a counterexample.
4. 🧱 (← 1) — Reachability chains together: if X reaches Y and Y reaches Z, then X reaches Z by following the two routes in turn.
5. 🚂 (← 1, 4) — Build a one-way line of three islands: bridge A→B and bridge B→H, and nothing else.
6. ✅ (← 2, 4, 5) — The givens hold: B reaches H directly, A reaches H through B, and H reaches itself.
7. 🌱 (← 5) — A is a pure starting point: no bridge points into A.
8. 🚫 (← 7) — Since no bridge enters A, no other island can ever reach A, so B and H cannot get back to A.
9. 🏁 (← 5) — H is a pure end point: no bridge leaves H, so H cannot reach A or B.
10. 🔒 (← 4, 8, 9) — Travel on the line only goes forward, so every backward pair (B to A, H to B, H to A) is unreachable.
11. 👀 (← 6) — Self-check: the network is finite, the bridges are one-way, and all three islands reach H, so the example respects the givens.
12. 🎯 (← 3, 6, 8, 10) — The givens hold but mutual reachability fails, so the guarantee is false.

LANDMARKS:
- Reachability is transitive, so a one-way line A→B→H lets everyone reach H while the order of the line can never be reversed.
- The start of the line is a source with no incoming bridge, so no other island can reach it. This is a second obstruction besides H having no exit.
- The hub condition constrains only the routes into H. It places no demand on incoming bridges for the other islands.

ANSWER: No. Counterexample: three islands A, B, H with bridges A→B and B→H only. Everyone reaches H, but H cannot reach B or A, and B cannot reach A.
