Question

Twenty-three people are in a room. **What is the probability that at least two of them were born in

Pigeonhole contradiction: twelve months cannot hold twenty-three distinctly VALID

sonnet (duelist A) · 2026-10-05 · strategy new: pigeonhole-certainty · depth 1 · opus/v1 / criterion v1
reason: Each step follows from its parents, and the answer of exactly 1 is correct by pigeonhole. Because the frontier is empty, all three landmarks are new: twenty-three people exceed twelve months, distinct months run out by the twelfth person, and a shared month occurs in every arrangement so the event is certain.
CHAIN by duelist A
1. 🧑‍🤝‍🧑 (root) — Given: twenty-three people are in a room.
2. 📅 (root) — Given from general knowledge: a calendar year has exactly twelve months, and everyone is born in exactly one of them.
3. ❓ (← 1, 2) — Asked: what is the probability that at least two of the twenty-three share a birth month? This is the month, not the day-of-year, as in the familiar birthday problem.
4. 🗄️ (← 2) — Picture twelve labelled boxes, one per month. Each person's birth month puts them in exactly one box.
5. 🚶 (← 1, 4) — Place the people into the boxes one at a time, each into the box for their own month.
6. 🧪 (← 2, 5) — Suppose, for contradiction, that nobody shares a month. Then every person occupies a different box, so the twelve boxes are used up after twelve people.
7. ⚖️ (← 1, 6) — Set the crowd against the boxes: twenty-three people is far more than twelve boxes, nearly double.
8. 🚪 (← 6, 7) — After the boxes are all taken, any further person must go into a box that is already occupied.
9. 💥 (← 6, 8) — This contradicts the supposition that everyone has a different month, so the supposition cannot hold.
10. 🔒 (← 9) — Therefore every possible set of birth months for this room contains a shared month. No arrangement avoids it, however unlikely it looks.
11. 🎯 (← 10) — An event that happens in every possible arrangement is certain, so its probability is the maximum, one hundred percent.
12. 👀 (← 3, 11) — Self-check: the question is about months, not days. The famous twenty-three, about even odds, applies to days, and does not apply here, where certainty holds.

LANDMARKS:
- There are only twelve possible birth months, and twenty-three people exceed that.
- Assuming all months are different would use up the twelve months by the twelfth person (pigeonhole), leaving the thirteenth with no new month.
- A shared month occurs in every possible arrangement, so the event is certain, with no need to weigh likelihoods.

ANSWER: 1 (100%, certain)

Complement via sequential avoidance chances reaching zero VALID

sonnet (duelist B) · 2026-10-05 · strategy new: complement-product · depth 2 · opus/v1 / criterion v1
reason: Every step is sound and the answer of 1 is exact. Landmarks 2 and 3 repeat route 001's pigeonhole and certainty claims, but landmark 1 is new: no earlier binding chain finds the complement by multiplying each arrival's chance of avoiding the earlier months, a chance that shrinks to zero at the thirteenth person.
CHAIN by duelist B
1. 🧑‍🤝‍🧑 (root) — Given: twenty-three people are in a room.
2. 📅 (root) — Given from general knowledge: a year has exactly twelve calendar months, and each person is born in exactly one of them.
3. ❓ (← 1, 2) — Asked: what is the probability that at least two of the twenty-three share a birth month? This is the month version, not the familiar day-of-year birthday problem.
4. 🔄 (← 3) — Use the complement. "At least two share a month" is the opposite of "everyone has a different month", so the two probabilities fill the whole certainty between them.
5. 🚶 (← 1, 4) — Bring the people in one at a time. For "all different" to hold, each newcomer must land in a month that nobody before them has.
6. 🟢 (← 2, 5) — Count the free months as people arrive. The first person has all twelve free, and each later arrival leaves one fewer free month than before.
7. 📉 (← 6) — The chance that a newcomer avoids all earlier months shrinks with every arrival, because the free months dwindle. It starts near certain and ends up small.
8. 🈳 (← 2, 6) — After twelve people with all different months, no month is left free. The count of free months has dropped to nothing.
9. 🚫 (← 7, 8) — So for the thirteenth person, the chance of landing in a free month is nothing. A newcomer cannot avoid a month that has no free option.
10. ⛓️ (← 5, 9) — "All different" needs every newcomer's step to succeed in turn. One step with no chance of success makes the whole chain's chance nothing.
11. ➕ (← 1, 10) — The room has twenty-three people, so it reaches the thirteenth person and beyond. The impossible step is therefore part of this room.
12. 🅾️ (← 4, 11) — The chance that all twenty-three have different months is zero.
13. 🎯 (← 4, 12) — The complement of a zero chance is full certainty, so a shared month has probability one.
14. 👀 (← 3, 13) — Self-check: this is about months, not days. The famous roughly even-odds result at twenty-three belongs to days, so it does not apply here.

LANDMARKS:
- For everyone to have different months, each new arrival must avoid all earlier months, and that chance shrinks as the free months get used up.
- After twelve people, no free month remains, so the chance the thirteenth avoids a match is nothing.
- The probability that all twenty-three months differ is zero, so its complement, a shared month, has probability one.

ANSWER: 1 (100%, certain)

Pigeonhole via one representative per occupied month DUPLICATE

"At most twelve representatives, so with twenty-three some are not representatives" is route 001's landmark 1: only twelve months, and twenty-three people exceed that. "A non-representative's month already has a different person in it" is route 001's landmark 2 in other words: once the twelve months are used up, the extra person has no new month. CHAIN by duelist B
1. 🧑‍🤝‍🧑 (root) — Given: twenty-three people are in a room.
2. 📅 (root) — Given from general knowledge: a year has exactly twelve calendar months, and each person is born in exactly one of them.
3. ❓ (← 1, 2) — Asked: what is the probability that at least two of the twenty-three share a birth month? This is the month version, not the familiar day-of-year birthday problem.
4. 🎖️ (← 2, 3) — For every month that appears among the birth months in the room, appoint one representative: a person born in that month. Each appearing month gets exactly one representative.
5. 🔢 (← 2, 4) — There is at most one representative per month, so there are at most twelve representatives in total.
6. 🧮 (← 1, 5) — Set the crowd against the representatives: twenty-three people is far more than twelve. So many people, at least nearly half the room, are not representatives.
7. 🙋 (← 6) — Pick any one person who is not a representative.
8. 🔗 (← 4, 7) — That person's birth month appears in the room, so it has a representative. That representative is a different person, because the one picked is not a representative.
9. 🤝 (← 7, 8) — The picked person and their month's representative are two different people born in the same month. So a shared month exists.
10. 🔒 (← 9) — This worked for any room of this size and any birth months, with no particular dates assumed. So every possible arrangement contains a shared month.
11. 🎯 (← 10) — An event that holds in every possible arrangement is certain, so its probability is one.
12. 👀 (← 3, 11) — Self-check: this is about months, not days. The famous roughly even-odds result at twenty-three belongs to days, and does not apply here.

LANDMARKS:
- Choosing one representative for each month that appears gives at most twelve representatives, so with twenty-three people some are not representatives.
- Every non-representative was born in a month that already has a representative, a different person, so the two of them share a month.
- This works for any set of birth months, so a shared month is certain.
