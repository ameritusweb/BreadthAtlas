Balancing nine eggs, a laptop, a book, an open wine bottle and a nail

family: physical design, open problem · status: open · attractor: the default stack (book, laptop, eggs on top) and its rearrangements

Question

You have nine eggs, a laptop, a book, an open wine bottle (part full) and a nail, and nothing else.
Arrange them, using only these objects, so that the whole arrangement resists toppling. Every listed
object — all nine eggs, the laptop, the book, the bottle and the nail — must be part of the
arrangement; setting any aside answers a different question. **Describe an arrangement and the
principle of stability it relies on.** Every route must rest on a genuinely
different principle, not a rearrangement of the same stack.

Glossary

Givens are the objects as physical facts, not labels:

| emoji | object | properties that matter |
|---|---|---|
| 🥚 | egg | ovoid; strong under compression along its axis; rolls on a flat surface; fragile to point loads |
| 💻 | laptop | hinged; opens to any angle; flat and rigid when closed; keyboard wells and lid lip |
| 📖 | book | rigid rectangle; uniform weight; can stand on an edge or be opened into a V |
| 🍷 | open wine bottle, part full | tall cylinder; top-heavy when upright; liquid shifts with tilt and settles low |
| 📌 | nail | thin rigid spike; concentrates force at a point; can wedge or pin |

Principles — a route's spine is one of these, or a new one the referee accepts:

| emoji | principle |
|---|---|
| ⚓ | low centre of mass: lay things down rather than stack |
| 🔺 | three-point contact / triangulation |
| 🔒 | interlocking: shapes that capture each other (bottle in the laptop hinge, eggs in keyboard wells) |
| 🌊 | liquid ballast: wine settles to the low side and resists tipping |
| ⚖️ | counterbalance across a fulcrum |
| 📐 | wedging: an edge or spike stops a roll |
| 🤝 | mutual support: objects that lean into each other, none stable alone |
| 🏔️ | pyramid: widest at the base |
| 🫙 | containment: an object that cannot be balanced is neutralized by enclosing it in another (the nail inside the bottle) — fewer things left to balance |

Mechanisms (steps, not spines): 🪨 sinks — denser than the liquid, settles to the bottom and stays
there under tilt; ⬇️ settles low.

Low centre of mass over wide flat base WRONG

reason: Step 13 (via 11) treats the loose stack as one rigid body, so it uses the laptop's wide base and the combined centre of mass. But the bottle stands free on the book and tips about its own narrow base, so the stated principle is not what keeps the weakest part of the arrangement upright.
CHAIN by blind player 1
1. 🥚 (root) — Given: nine eggs.
2. 💻 (root) — Given: a laptop.
3. 📖 (root) — Given: a book.
4. 🍷 (root) — Given: an open wine bottle, part full.
5. 🔩 (root) — Given: a nail, and nothing else is available.
6. ❓ (← 1, 2, 3, 4, 5) — Asked: arrange these objects so the whole arrangement resists toppling, and name the stability principle it relies on.
7. ⚖️ (root) — General fact: a body resists tipping when the vertical line through its center of mass falls well inside its support base, because tipping would have to raise that center first.
8. 💻 (← 2, 6, 7) — Lay the closed laptop flat on a table as the bottom layer: it is wide, flat and fairly heavy, so it gives a large base that is very low.
9. 📖 (← 3, 8) — Lay the book flat on the middle of the laptop as a level platform, which adds little height and spreads the load over the base.
10. 🍷 (← 4, 9) — Stand the bottle upright at the center of the book. Its liquid sits at the bottom, so the bottle's own center of mass is low, and it is the only tall object.
11. 📐 (← 7, 8, 10) — The combined center of mass is therefore low and over the middle of the base, far from every edge of the laptop.
12. 🥚🔩 (← 1, 5, 11) — Leave the eggs and nail out of the tall part. They rest separately on the table or laptop beside the bottle, so they add no height and cannot raise the center.
13. 🛡️ (← 7, 11, 12) — Tipping the stack needs a large tilt that lifts the center of mass before it can pass the base edge, so a small push just settles back.
14. 👀 (← 10, 13) — Self-check: if the bottle were nearly empty its center would be higher, but still low relative to the wide base, so the conclusion holds.

LANDMARKS:
- A wide, flat, low base (laptop plus book) is the foundation.
- Partly filled bottle: the liquid lowers the center of mass.
- Center of mass sits over the middle of the base, so tipping requires lifting it.

ANSWER: Lay the laptop flat, the book flat on it, and stand the part-full bottle upright in the center, with the eggs and nail kept low beside it. This is stable because the combined center of mass is low and lies well inside a wide support base.

CHAIN by blind player 2
1. 🥚 (root) — Given: nine eggs, which are oval and roll if placed loose on a flat surface.
2. 💻 (root) — Given: a laptop whose two halves are joined by a hinge, so it can be opened to any angle.
3. 📖 (root) — Given: a book, a stiff rectangular object with a spine.
4. 🍾 (root) — Given: an open, part-full wine bottle and a nail, and nothing else may be used.
5. ❓ (← 1, 2, 3, 4) — Asked: arrange all of these so the arrangement resists toppling, and name the stability principle. The route must differ from a simple low, wide stack.
6. 📐 (root) — General fact: a triangle cannot change shape without bending a side, so triangular frames are rigid and resist sideways racking and collapse.
7. 💻 (← 2, 6) — Open the laptop to about 70 degrees and stand it on its two outer edges as an inverted V. The two panels and the table form a triangle, so the tent braces itself against sideways pushing.
8. 📖 (← 3, 6) — Open the book and stand it as a second, smaller tent beside the laptop. Its two covers also form a triangle, which gives a second braced arch.
9. 🥚 (← 1, 7, 8) — Line the eggs in the hollow under the tents, wedged against the panels' inner faces. The slanted walls stop them rolling, and the eggs sit as low weights inside the frames.
10. 🍾 (← 4, 7) — Lay the nail across the two tents' tops as a tie bar, and rest the bottle's weight on the table beneath the hinge line. The tie bar links the two triangles, so one cannot spread without the other.
11. 🔺 (← 6, 7, 8, 10) — Load on any one panel is carried along the slanted panel to the table and to the opposite panel, not turned into a tipping moment. So a push cannot fold the structure sideways without deforming a rigid triangle.
12. 👀 (← 11) — Self-check: the tents resist spreading only if friction at the feet stops the legs sliding out. The eggs, bottle and book edges on the table supply it, so the claim holds.

LANDMARKS:
- A laptop opened as an inverted V forms a triangle with the table, and a triangle is a rigid shape.
- Stability here comes from structural bracing (triangulation), not from a low center of mass or a wide flat base.
- Slanted panels hold the eggs in place and pass loads down to the table.
- A nail tie bar couples two braced tents so neither can spread alone.

ANSWER: Stand the laptop half-open as an A-frame tent and the book as a second tent beside it. Wedge the eggs under them, tie the tents together with the nail, and set the bottle on the table beneath. It resists toppling by triangulation: braced triangular frames are rigid and carry load down to the table without tipping.
